If there's one thing I've learnt from the long list of "projects" I've taken up since my childhood which now remain completely abondoned is that before starting, you must have a strong why. If there isn't, why bother starting at all? Starting a blog is something I've wanted to wanted to do for a very long time and now that I've started one, I thought why not make the first post about the why.

A paragragh in [Genius: The Life and Science of Richard Feynman](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/98685.Genius) has stuck with me since I read it:
> On the title page he wrote: Notebook Of Things I Don’t Know About. For the first but not the last time he reorganized his knowledge. He worked for weeks at disassembling each branch of physics, oiling the parts, and putting them back together, looking all the while for the raw edges and inconsistencies. He tried to find the essential kernels of each subject.

With this blog, I wish to revisit some of the concepts that I take for granted in my job or didn't pay much attention to in my bachelor's degree. I'm hoping that by forcing myself to put into words my understanding of various concepts and publishing these words publicly, I hold myself to a higher standard and really dig deep. This also applies to all the new concepts that I will inevitably have to learn.

Another reason is [Paul Graham](https://paulgraham.com/writes.html) seems to think:
> The reason so many people have trouble writing is that it's fundamentally difficult. To write well you have to think clearly, and thinking clearly is hard. [...] writing is thinking. In fact there's a kind of thinking that can only be done by writing.

I don't know how exaggerated these statements are. But it has nevertheless convinced me to alteast give writing a shot. Needless to say, I will not be using any LLM to do the ~writing~ thinking for me.
